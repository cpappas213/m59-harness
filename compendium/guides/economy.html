Money, Merchants and the Economy

Every item in the game has one declared value, and every price a merchant quotes is that number run through two multipliers: a markup band set per merchant, and — for weapons and armour — the item's own condition. A normal shopkeeper sells at 160% of value and buys at 70%, so the spread is a factor of 2.3. Where you shop matters more than what you carry.

The two price formulas

what you PAY: price = viValue_average × (100 + 20 × markup) / 100 × faction price bonus / 100 bounded to at least 1 what you are OFFERED: offer = GetValue() × (100 − 10 × markup) / 100 bounded to at least 1

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/monster.kod:4880 GetPrice · :3137 (the buying-from-you path)

markup is a small integer from 0 to 5 declared per merchant. Note the asymmetry: it is worth 20 points per step when you buy and only 10 when you sell.

Buying from a ripoff merchant and selling back to them loses you 75% of the item's value in one round trip. Buying from a bargain merchant and selling to a different bargain merchant loses 25%. Over a career the difference is enormous, and nothing in the game tells you which is which — the NPC list does.

The four bargain merchants are Ravi, Tomas, Quintor and Skivlat. The nine ripoff merchants include Esseldi, Roq, Rook, Zhieu B'hob, Xacal ko'Ixca and Cital ko'Manca — note how many of those are in outlying or hostile territory. You pay for convenience.

Condition is price, for anything that wears out

What you are offered uses GetValue, not the flat average, and that function multiplies by a condition term that is quadratic in the item's history:

percent = 100 × piHits_init × piHits / viHits_init_max² value = viValue_average × percent / 100 bounded to [10, viValue_average]

kod/object/item.kod:408 GetValue, :403 GetInitValue

piHits_init is the maximum this particular item rolled when it was created, and piHits is its current condition. Both divide by the class maximum, so the two losses compound:

the curve is drawn out in the comment at kod/object/item.kod:414–430

Two practical consequences. A fresh item that happened to roll a low maximum is permanently worth less, however carefully you treat it — and there is no way to see the roll except by comparing prices. And selling worn gear is close to worthless: a half-worn item at a normal merchant returns 70% of 48%, about a third of what you paid, before the markup on what you buy next.

Mending restores piHits but not piHits_init — the first term of that product never recovers. Items say so themselves: armour "in great condition, but has been repaired before" is a different string from armour "without blemish or flaw."

kod/object/item/passitem/defmod/armor.kod:19–20

Where money comes from

Almost all of it comes off corpses. Money is not a separate roll on a treasure table — it is an ordinary weighted row, competing with every other drop:

shillings = 1 + (MoneyFactor × 2 × bound(random(level/2, 3 × level/2), 1, ∞)) / 100 with MoneyFactor = 100, so roughly level/2 … 3×level/2 ×2

kod/object/passive/trestype.kod:302 · MoneyFactor at kod/util/settings.kod

So a level-80 creature that rolls the money row yields about 81–241 shillings, and the chance of rolling that row is whatever weight the table gives it — 2% for a cave orc. Coin is a rare drop, not a reliable one. The reliable income is selling what does drop, which is why the markup band of the merchant you sell to is the single biggest lever on your income.

How many items a corpse yields at all:

rolls = 1 if MOB_ONE_TREASURE = bound(1 + level/55 + random(0, difficulty/3), $, 6) otherwise then × ItemFactor / 100, floored at 1

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/monster.kod:4964, :4973

Difficulty is what makes a corpse rich, not level: a level-100 creature at difficulty 0 rolls twice, and a level-40 creature at difficulty 9 rolls up to four times. Sort the bestiary by difficulty to find the profitable fights.

Faction pricing

One faction gets a discount, and only one:

if your faction is not Princess: multiplier = 100 (no change) Princess soldiers: a percentage change in your favour, both directions

kod/util/parlia.kod:1144 GetFactionPriceBonus

The bonus applies to what you pay. It is stated in the source as around 5%, and it scales with the faction's standing like every other faction benefit — see Factions.

Who will not trade with you

Merchants flagged MOB_LAWFUL refuse to do business publicly with murderers. Faction pressure can override that refusal, which is one of the quieter arguments for joining a side.

kod/include/blakston.khd:1407 · kod/util/parlia.kod:1468 GetFactionSellToMurderer

Some merchants are MOB_COND_SELLER: they have goods that must be asked for by name rather than browsed. If a shop's list looks short, try naming the thing you want.

kod/include/blakston.khd:1405

Fixed prices

A merchant may override the formula entirely. If plFor_Sale has a fourth element, it is a list of [item, price] pairs, and those prices are returned unmodified — no markup, no faction bonus, no condition term.

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/monster.kod:4886–4895

Spells and skills are also exempt: GetPrice returns the ability's own GetValue with no markup at all. A teacher who is a ripoff merchant for goods charges the standard rate for tuition.

kod/object/active/holder/nomoveon/battler/monster.kod:4904–4907

Practical advice

  • Sell to bargain merchants, buy from bargain merchants. Ravi, Tomas, Quintor and Skivlat. The difference against a ripoff merchant is a factor of three on the round trip.
  • Do not carry worn gear home to sell. Condition is quadratic; below about half condition an item is worth a tenth of new, and the weight and bulk are better spent on reagents or gems.
  • Gems and reagents hold their value — they have no condition term, so they sell at exactly value × (100 − 10 × markup) / 100. They are the efficient way to carry wealth out of a dungeon.
  • Fight difficulty, not level, for loot. The number of drops keys off difficulty; the value of each keys off the creature's treasure table.
  • Check the fixed-price list. Some things are priced outside the formula and are bargains or traps regardless of the merchant's band.
